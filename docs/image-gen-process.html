How a picture gets made

From a source photo to a picture that passes 30 checks

This page walks through the whole image generation process of nano-banana-facecard: which route to take, how the Gemini session works, what each pipeline does step by step, which numbers decide pass or fail, and how a picture earns the word "best".

The idea in one glance

Four parties take part. Each does only the job it is good at, and no one marks their own work.

The rule everything else follows: nothing Gemini renders is altered by a heuristic. In round 2 a face mask, a relight, added grain and a landmark warp sat on top of Gemini's crop, and the kayak photo's identity score fell from 0.58 to 0.23. Those steps understood the picture less well than Gemini did, so they were removed. Code now only copies pixels that must not change, measures, and retries.

How it talks to Gemini

There is no browser and no API key. A small client logs in with two cookies from your encrypted vault and sends the same requests the Gemini web app sends.

      Credits: what one thing costs

      Gemini charges compute credits, not requests. Image and video draw from the same pool. These are measured costs on your Pro account, not Google's published prices. The estimate is only accurate when nothing else uses the account at the same time, because the meter is account-wide.

      Per call: text 1 to 2, video about 520. Video is the same price whichever image model is chosen.
      Check the meter before a wave: .venv\Scripts\python.exe tools\quota.py --watch keeps one line updated and writes .cache\quota.json. When the window is empty the work does not stop, it waits for the reset time.

      Route A: replicate, your own photo

      It edits a crop of the head and pastes it back into the untouched original. The background, clothes, body and framing cannot drift because those pixels are never sent for editing. Step through it.

      Route B: gen --goal, a pin or someone else's photo
      
  
      Here the picture is built, not edited in place, so more can drift. That is why this route measures the goal first, lists every element before the first render, and audits by hand after it.
      
  
      
  
      Why the other man's photo is handled so carefully: photos sent to the generator pass their model's skull, jaw and neck into the output (measured 2026-09-19, even with the face blurred). Putting your head on his body kept his silhouette within 2 percent and about 15 percent of results were acceptable. The default is therefore text plus measurements. With --replace-subject the pin is sent as the photo to edit, and his whole man is replaced by you from your own references.
      

      


      
  
      The 30-item audit
      
  
      No picture is called best until it passes every item below, checked by eye with the head zoomed in next to the source. Every item comes from a complaint you made. Click an item to cycle it: pass, fail, not applicable. This is the same logic tools\best_check.py records.
